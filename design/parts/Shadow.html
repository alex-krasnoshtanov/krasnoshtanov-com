The person is outside the frame. Their shadow isn't.

Given a frame where the pedestrian is entirely out of shot, predict the box they occupy anyway. Re‑describing that box against the edge it hides behind — which side, how far out, how wide, how tall — took mean IoU from 0.4675 to 0.6096, because a distance and a width cannot describe an impossible box the way two loose corners can.

Nobody is ever clipped by the frame edge, so which side they are on has no ambiguous middle case. One mean box for all 1,692 training frames scores 0.0000 — the boxes sit on both sides. Splitting by side is what creates a floor at all, and it is the same idea the model needed.
